\title{Hierarchical Constraint-Aware Design-Space Exploration for\\
Heterogeneous Distributed Cyber-Physical Systems}

\author[lp]{Nikita Tarasov}
\author[lp]{Vasyl Tomyuk}
\ead{dev.nikita@outlook.com}
\address[lp]{Lviv Polytechnic National University, Lviv, Ukraine}

\begin{abstract}
Architecting a heterogeneous distributed cyber-physical system requires simultaneous
decisions on the number and class of edge and gateway nodes, on where application
stages are executed, on replication, and on communication links, under conflicting
latency, energy, cost and network-volume objectives. The resulting design space grows
combinatorially, and for distributed cyber-physical systems a single design point is
expensive to evaluate, so exhaustive exploration is impractical while population-based
search returns approximate fronts without guarantees. This paper presents HCA-DSE, a
hierarchical constraint-aware design-space exploration method that discards structurally
ill-formed, provably infeasible and provably dominated regions of the architecture space
\emph{before} any full design-point evaluation. Its central technical ingredient is a
family of admissible objective bounds for \emph{contention-dependent} objectives: the
end-to-end latency of a distributed cyber-physical system depends on tier utilisation,
which is not determined by any single design decision, so the assignment-monotonicity
assumed by existing exact exploration methods does not hold. We prove that the resulting
pruning is safe and that the exact Pareto front is preserved, and we verify both the
bound admissibility and the front equality exhaustively on reference spaces. On four
design-space scales, three workload classes and spaces of up to $8.3\times10^{7}$
candidate architectures, HCA-DSE performs 26--61 full evaluations instead of $10^{3}$ to
$10^{7}$, preserves the exhaustive Pareto front exactly (recall and precision of $1.0$),
and visits fewer than $2\times10^{-4}$ of the candidates while never materialising the
design space. Budget-matched NSGA-II requires 1000 evaluations to reach a median Pareto
recall of $0.50$--$0.91$. An evaluation-cost analysis places the measured break-even
evaluator cost at $1$--$3\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ on the evaluated hardware and design spaces;
using the measured $19.4$\,ms cost of a discrete-event evaluator, the cost model
projects speed-ups of up to $558\times$.
Finally, the analytical model is validated against a containerised edge--gateway--cloud
testbed with network shaping: over 36 architectures and 108 measured runs, the model
orders architectures consistently with the measurements in $98\%$ of the pairs it
separates by more than $25\%$.
\end{abstract}
