\title{Hierarchical Constraint-Aware Design-Space Exploration for\\
Heterogeneous Distributed Cyber-Physical Systems}

\author[lp]{Nikita Tarasov}
\ead{dev.nikita@outlook.com}
\author[lp]{Vasyl Tomyuk}
\address[lp]{Lviv Polytechnic National University, Lviv, Ukraine}

\begin{abstract}
Architecting a heterogeneous distributed cyber-physical system requires simultaneous
decisions on the number and class of edge and gateway nodes, on where application
stages are executed, on replication, and on communication links, under conflicting
latency, energy, cost and network-volume objectives. The resulting design space grows
combinatorially, and for distributed cyber-physical systems a single design point is
expensive to evaluate, so exhaustive exploration is impractical while population-based
search returns approximate fronts without guarantees. This paper presents HCA-DSE, a
hierarchical constraint-aware design-space exploration method that discards structurally
ill-formed, provably infeasible and provably dominated regions of the architecture space
without fully evaluating each discarded candidate. Its central technical ingredient is a
family of objective-specific admissible relaxations for a finite heterogeneous edge--cloud CPS model with \emph{contention-dependent} objectives: the
end-to-end latency of a distributed cyber-physical system depends on tier utilisation,
which is not determined by any single design decision, so a sum of assigned decision costs is not by itself an admissible bound. We prove that the resulting
pruning is safe and that the exact Pareto front is preserved, and we verify both the
bound admissibility and the front equality exhaustively on reference spaces. Across four design-space scales and a scalability sweep up to $8.3\times10^{7}$
candidate architectures, the default method performs $\EvalRange$ full evaluations.
It preserves the exhaustive analytical Pareto front on the reference spaces S1--S3;
exactness on the largest spaces rests on the bound proof. Budget-matched NSGA-II attains
median Pareto recall of $\BaselineRecallRange$ at 1000 actual evaluator calls.
An evaluation-cost model anchored at a measured DES cost of $\DesCostMs$\,ms projects
speed-ups up to $\ProjectionMax\times$; this is not an end-to-end DES search measurement.
We additionally isolate variable-order and queue-bound effects and compare against an
exact finite-table SMT baseline. An independent containerised validation covers 36
architectures and 108 runs with the model frozen. For architecture pairs separated by
more than $25\%$ in predicted latency, agreement with measured mean-latency order is
$\WithinAgreementRange\%$ within workloads ($\PooledAgreement\%$ pooled).
\end{abstract}
