\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Internal validity}
The correctness of the method rests on the admissibility of the bounds. We address this
at three levels: proofs (Lemmas~\ref{lem:relax}--\ref{lem:exact},
Propositions~\ref{prop:feas}--\ref{prop:struct}, Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}); an exhaustive
machine check of $\LB_i(p)\le f_i(x)$ over all prefixes and all completions on S1 for the
three workloads; and a direct check that, for every pruned prefix, no completion
contradicts the claim of the corresponding rule. We deliberately separate \emph{theoretical}
correctness from \emph{implementation} correctness and verify both, since an admissible
bound that is implemented incorrectly is indistinguishable, in the output, from an
inadmissible one. Structural pruning is part of the definition of the design space rather
than a consequence of the constraints, and the exhaustive baseline uses the same
predicate; this was discovered by the pruning-safety test and is stated explicitly rather
than absorbed into the comparison.

\paragraph{Post-hoc model correction}
The replacement of the M/M/1 waiting term by the M/D/1 expression was motivated by the
testbed measurements and evaluated on the same dataset. The correction is theoretically
justified independently of the data --- stage execution is deterministic by construction
--- but the reported improvement in MAPE is an in-sample consistency check, not an
out-of-sample validation, and is labelled as such in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{Construct validity}
The analytical model abstracts a real deployment: a tier is modelled as one M/D/1 station
with the aggregate capacity of its nodes rather than as an M/D/$c$ system, transport
protocol effects are ignored, the power model is linear in utilisation, and costs are
normalised units. The aggregation is optimistic by construction, and the testbed --- whose
nodes are genuinely single-server containers --- is what quantifies its cost. The testbed validation quantifies
the resulting gap and shows that it affects absolute prediction much more than ordering.
The sensing workload exposes the main structural gap --- for large payloads over a shaped
link the model does not capture transport-level effects and over-predicts systematically.
The energy and cost models are not validated against measurements at all; they are used
for comparative exploration only, and no absolute energy or cost claim is made.

\paragraph{External validity}
The workloads are synthetic, although chosen to be qualitatively different rather than
rescaled versions of one another, and the architecture template is fixed to four tiers
with three stages. The empirical study is further restricted to configurations that fit on
a single machine ($\le4$ edge nodes, $\le2$ gateways), and link shaping is applied in the
forward direction of each hop, matching the one-way transfers counted by the model. Results
on a geographically distributed deployment may differ, particularly for the WAN hop.

\paragraph{Conclusion validity}
Deterministic methods (exhaustive, HCA-DSE) require no statistical treatment. Stochastic
baselines are run with 20 independent seeds and compared with non-parametric tests
(Mann--Whitney $U$, Cliff's $\delta$). Testbed configurations are repeated three times and
reported by their median; the four configurations whose repeats differ by more than a
factor of $1.5$ are reported separately rather than discarded, and all four operate at
$\rho\ge0.75$.

\paragraph{Limitations}
The method assumes a static workload during one exploration, does not model runtime
migration or autoscaling, and requires bounds to be derived per objective --- a manual
step that must be repeated when the objective set changes. No comparison against an exact symbolic or
mixed-integer formulation is provided. Exact integer programming is demonstrably
practical for allocation problems in this domain~\cite{kouloumpris2024optimization,
kouloumpris2026reliability}, but those formulations are linear in the decision variables,
whereas the waiting term $\rho_t/(2(1-\rho_t))$ of \eqref{eq:latency} is not: a
mixed-integer formulation of our problem would require linearising it and would therefore
solve a different model exactly rather than ours. The same holds for symbolic exploration
under assignment monotonicity~\cite{neubauer2018exact}. The question of relative
efficiency against a solver working on a linearised surrogate therefore remains open, and
we consider it the most valuable single addition to this line of work.
