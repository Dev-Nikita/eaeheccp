\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Internal validity}
The correctness of the method rests on the admissibility of the bounds. We address this
at three levels: proofs (Lemmas~\ref{lem:relax}--\ref{lem:exact},
Propositions~\ref{prop:feas}--\ref{prop:struct}, Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}); an exhaustive
machine check of $\LB_i(p)\le f_i(x)$ over all prefixes and all completions on S1 for the
three workloads; and a direct check that, for every pruned prefix, no completion
contradicts the claim of the corresponding rule. We deliberately separate \emph{theoretical}
correctness from \emph{implementation} correctness and verify both, since an admissible
bound that is implemented incorrectly is indistinguishable, in the output, from an
inadmissible one. Structural pruning is part of the definition of the design space rather
than a consequence of the constraints, and the exhaustive baseline uses the same
predicate; this was discovered by the pruning-safety test and is stated explicitly rather
than absorbed into the comparison.

\paragraph{Post-hoc model correction}
Earlier measurements motivated the M/D/1-inspired waiting correction. The independent
second round freezes this model and all 36 configurations beforehand, so its results are
not in-sample calibration scores. Selection of these configurations still reflects the
original study, and a single-machine repeat is not an independent deployment population.
The idealised queueing argument does not determine the sign of deployment error.

\paragraph{Construct validity}
The analytical model uses per-node service times with a stage-wise M/D/1-inspired
waiting multiplier based on average tier utilisation. It is neither an exact
aggregate-server queue nor an exact multi-server queue, and its error has no
proved sign. Transport protocol effects are ignored, the power model is linear
in utilisation, and costs are normalised units. Testbed validation quantifies
latency error and ordering agreement, but does not prove admissibility for a
simulator or for the deployment.
The model omits transport-level and host-scheduling effects, which can be especially consequential for large payloads and near saturation.
The energy and cost models are not validated against measurements at all; they are used
for comparative exploration only, and no absolute energy or cost claim is made.

\paragraph{External validity}
The workloads are synthetic, although chosen to be qualitatively different rather than
rescaled versions of one another, and the architecture template is fixed to four tiers
with three stages. The empirical study is further restricted to configurations that fit on
a single machine ($\le4$ edge nodes, $\le2$ gateways), and link shaping is applied in the
forward direction of each hop, matching the one-way transfers counted by the model. Results
on a geographically distributed deployment may differ, particularly for the WAN hop.

\paragraph{Conclusion validity}
Search counts are deterministic for fixed inputs and order; wall-clock timings still
vary. Stochastic baselines use 20 seeds and exploratory, unadjusted Mann--Whitney tests
with Cliff's $\delta$. Testbed configurations have only three repeats. We report the
median of run means, repeat IQR and a largest/smallest ratio; unstable configurations
remain in the main results. This sample does not characterise tail-risk probabilities.

\paragraph{Evaluation-budget accounting}
All stochastic runs stop at exactly $B$ full evaluator calls, with structurally rejected
proposals counted separately. Calls returning infeasibility consume budget, as do repeat
evaluations. Thus budgets match full invocations rather than unique feasible designs or
wall-clock work. NSGA-II duplicate elimination and random sampling with replacement
remain distinct search choices, not equal unique-design budgets.

\paragraph{Limitations}
The method assumes a static workload during one exploration, does not model runtime
migration or autoscaling, and requires bounds to be derived per objective --- a manual
step that must be repeated when the objective set changes. The finite-table Z3 baseline on S1/S2 is an exact encoding, but fully evaluates the space
before solving. It does not establish superiority over compact SMT, CP-SAT or mixed-integer
formulations with native propagation. Exact integer allocation is practical for related
problems~\cite{kouloumpris2024optimization,kouloumpris2026reliability}; a comparable
compact encoding of this architecture model remains future work. These limits constrain
claims of general algorithmic efficiency beyond the measured competitors.
