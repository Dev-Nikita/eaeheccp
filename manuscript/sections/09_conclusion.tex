\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}
In the class of heterogeneous dCPS design spaces studied here, exploration cost is
strongly driven by full design-point evaluation cost as well as combinatorial space size.
HCA-DSE combines structural, feasibility and dominance pruning with objective-specific
admissible relaxations for a finite edge--cloud architecture model. The construction
preserves the exact analytical Pareto front; safe pruning and evaluator avoidance are
established principles, while the contribution here is their concrete bound construction
and experimental assessment for this model.

The default method performs $\EvalRange$ full evaluations across the reported spaces,
including a sweep up to $8.3\times10^7$ raw candidates. Exhaustive reference comparisons
on S1--S3 recover identical unrounded objective-vector sets. Stochastic baselines now
spend precisely their stated full-call budgets; at $B=1000$, NSGA-II median recall ranges
from $\BaselineRecallRange$. The cost model projects up to $\ProjectionMax\times$
speed-up using the measured $\DesCostMs$\,ms DES cost, without claiming an end-to-end
DES search measurement. The order/queue ablation distinguishes early bound activation
from traversal effects, and the finite-table SMT comparison provides a small-space exact
reference whose table-construction cost is explicitly included.

The independent Docker round uses 36 fixed architectures and 108 runs without refitting
the model. Agreement with measured mean-latency order is $\WithinAgreementRange\%$
within workloads for pairs separated by more than $25\%$ in the model, compared with
$\PooledAgreement\%$ pooled. These measurements assess transfer of analytical rankings;
they neither validate energy/cost objectives nor establish deployment-level Pareto
exactness. Further work should develop compact symbolic solver encodings and tighter
bounds proved admissible for higher-fidelity evaluators.
