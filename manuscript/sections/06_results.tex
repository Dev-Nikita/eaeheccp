\subsection{RQ2: the exact front is preserved}
For every scale for which exhaustive enumeration is tractable (S1--S3) and for every
workload, the set of objective vectors returned by HCA-DSE is identical to the exhaustive
Pareto front: recall and precision are both $1.0$. The same holds for each ablated
variant, confirming that no individual pruning layer is unsafe. Bound admissibility was
verified exhaustively on S1 --- all prefixes against all completions --- without a single
violation, and for every pruned prefix on S1 and S2 all completions were enumerated and
found consistent with the claim of the corresponding proposition.
Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto} shows the resulting front for S3/telemetry in two projections.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]

\subsection{Variable order and early queueing contributions}
\label{sec:order}
\input{generated/order}
On S3, O2 reduces expanded states in each workload, but uses two more full evaluations
than O1 for telemetry and control, and the same number for sensing. Within O2, enabling
the queue bound reduces full evaluations for all three workloads; its extra computation
means that this need not reduce runtime with the cheap analytical evaluator. Thus early
queueing pruning is demonstrable, while the best traversal depends on the cost metric.

\subsection{Independent validation after model correction}
Earlier deployment measurements motivated replacing an M/M/1-inspired waiting term
with the M/D/1-inspired expression. This historical change was post hoc; the independent
round reported here uses a frozen model and is not fitted to its observations.
Deterministic service motivates the factor of two only in an idealised single-server
queue. It does not establish an exact queueing formula for the full deployment, so we
retain the measured errors and unstable repeats as limitations rather than adjusting
the model after validation.
